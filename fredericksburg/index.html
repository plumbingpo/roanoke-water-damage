What Usually Goes Wrong in Fredericksburg Homes

Most water damage calls we take in Fredericksburg trace back to a short list of causes. Knowing them makes it easier to catch trouble while it is still a small repair.

Pipes: When water freezes inside a line it can crack the metal or plastic, then pour out as soon as things warm up. Depending on the season, the plumbing most at risk runs through outside walls, attics and garages without heat. Corroded fittings and worn supply lines can also let go without any warning in older homes. Your first move is closing the main water valve, since a broken pipe floods room after room fast.

Appliances: The thin hoses feeding a washer, dishwasher or ice maker do not last forever. A water heater near the end of its life can leak from the tank bottom or fail all at once. These failures do the most harm when the appliance is on an upper floor or above finished living space.

Overflows: It takes only a few minutes for a blocked toilet or a forgotten faucet to flood a bathroom. From there it slips beneath the trim and can drip into whatever sits under that floor. If the overflow came from the toilet bowl or a drain, treat it as contaminated and keep children and pets away.

Roof: Rain gets in through worn flashing, damaged shingles or gutters that have stopped draining. By the time a brown ring appears overhead, the attic insulation has often been wet for a while. We dry the framing, insulation and drywall, and a roofer needs to fix the opening so it does not return.

Basement: Once the ground outside is soaked, water finds its way into a basement through wall cracks and the seam where floor meets wall. If the sump pump sticks or the electricity goes out mid-storm, the pit fills and spills across the slab. Finished basements suffer most, since carpet, drywall and framing all soak up water from the bottom.

Mold: Mold can start growing within 24 to 48 hours on materials that stay wet. Many mold jobs we handle in Fredericksburg began with an old leak that was wiped up but never dried inside the wall. A musty smell, peeling paint or dark spotting near a previous repair is worth having checked.
